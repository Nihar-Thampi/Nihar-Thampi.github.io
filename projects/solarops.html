SolarOps

Maintenance and job tracking for companies that service solar installations, with AI built into the daily work.

What it is
Multi-tenant web app

FastAPI, PostgreSQL, local LLM via Ollama

What it’s for

A solar operations and maintenance company looks after hundreds of installations for many clients, with crews spread across regions. Requests arrive by email, text and phone, some services recur every few months, and every visit should leave a record the customer can trust.

That usually lives in spreadsheets, inboxes and memory. SolarOps puts it in one place: clients, their sites, the recurring services each site needs, and every job from the first request to a signed-off service report, which becomes the site’s maintenance history. Customers rate each visit from a link, and AI turns those reviews into something a manager can act on.

Admins see the whole company. Managers see only the regions they run. Technicians see their own jobs, plus unassigned work in their region that they can pick up.

How a job moves through it

  1. A request comes in

    A manager pastes the customer’s email, text or call notes into AI intake. The local model drafts the job: service type, priority, the matching client and site, and contact details. Nothing is created until the manager reviews it and clicks Create job.

  2. Someone is assigned

    Suggested technicians are ranked by matching skills and how much open work they already have. A job can also stay unassigned for technicians in that region to pick up.

  3. The work happens

    The job moves from new to scheduled, in progress, on hold and completed, with an activity timeline of every change and note.

  4. The technician writes it up

    Rough field notes, written the way you would on site, plus optional readings, become a customer-ready report with a summary, work performed, findings and recommendations. The technician edits it and finalises it, and it prints or saves as a PDF.

  5. The customer rates the visit

    A link goes to the customer after the visit. Every review is tagged with sentiment and themes, and on demand AI summarises what customers value, their recurring complaints and suggested actions.

  6. The next visit is already scheduled

    If the job came from a recurring service, completing it rolls that service’s next due date forward, and the site’s maintenance history gains another final report.

What it does

Operations

  • Separate companies on one system, each with its data fully isolated
  • Work split by region, with Admin, Manager and Technician roles
  • Jobs with priorities, due dates, statuses, notes and filters by region, assignee, priority and service
  • Recurring services per site, such as panel cleaning every 90 days
  • Maintenance history for every site

AI features

  • Job intake from customer emails, texts and call notes
  • Service reports drafted from field notes
  • Sentiment and themes on every review, with summaries on demand
  • An AI activity log with each call’s input, output, model, latency, tokens and whether it fell back

Audit log

  • Every change, sign-in, failed sign-in and refused request: who, what, which record, before and after values, IP address and device
  • Append-only, enforced by database triggers
  • Hash-chained per company, with a one-click integrity check
  • Filters and CSV export, both of which are logged too

Dashboard

  • Open, unassigned and overdue work at a glance
  • Services coming due with no job yet
  • Weekly throughput, technician workload and a breakdown by region
  • Customer sentiment, and a table view for every chart

Decisions and trade-offs

AI output is always a draft

The model never changes data on its own. Whatever it produces is shown to a person, who accepts or edits it. Any ID the model picks, such as which client a message is about, is checked against what that user is allowed to see before it’s used.

A local model, held to a schema

The AI runs on Qwen2.5 through Ollama on the same machine, so customer messages and site details never leave it and there’s no cost per call. Constrained decoding means the model can only return JSON in the shape each feature expects, and the context window is set explicitly so long prompts, like the full client list for intake, are never silently cut.

It still works when the model doesn’t

If Ollama isn’t running, the model isn’t downloaded or a call fails, each feature falls back to rule-based logic. The top bar shows which mode is active, and the AI log records every fallback.

Scoping in one place

Company and region filtering happens in a single dependency that every route uses, so a new endpoint can’t forget it. Tests sign in as each role and as a second company to prove the isolation holds.

An audit log you can prove

Database triggers reject edits and deletes, and each entry stores a hash of the one before it. Verifying the chain shows that nothing was altered, removed or reordered. Even viewing and exporting the log is logged.
