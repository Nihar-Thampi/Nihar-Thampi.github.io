What it’s for

Invoices and receipts arrive as photos and scans. Getting them into something you can track means typing every vendor, date and line item by hand, and splitting a shared bill fairly means doing arithmetic over who had what.

FairShare reads the document for you. Upload a photo or scan and OCR plus an LLM extract the vendor, invoice number, dates, currency, printed total and every line item, with tax lines marked. You then track what’s unpaid or overdue, export to CSV, and when a bill is shared, split it item by item between the people who had each thing.

From photo to split

  1. Upload

    Drop in a photo or scan. Multi-page invoices work, and the file is saved under a random name.

  2. Read the text

    EasyOCR reads the image locally. Its text boxes are grouped into lines by vertical position, after correcting for the median tilt of the photo, so a skewed picture doesn’t pair an item with the wrong price.

  3. Structure it

    GPT-4o mini turns the raw text into structured JSON in JSON mode at low temperature, following rules for quantity, rate and total columns, wrapped item names and which rows to exclude. Malformed JSON gets an automatic repair retry.

  4. Clean it up

    A deterministic pipeline of about ten small functions fixes the failures I kept seeing: the rupee sign read as a “2”, item names split into fragments, bogus tax lines and impossible quantities, which are re-derived from total over unit price.

  5. Check the total

    The extracted items are added up and compared with the total printed on the invoice. A difference shows as a warning, so a missed or misread line gets caught.

  6. Review and track

    Every field can be corrected, and a rescan only fills empty fields so it never overwrites your edits. Mark invoices paid or unpaid; unpaid ones past their due date show as overdue.

  7. Split, if it’s shared

    Add the people, assign each item to one or more of them, and calculate. The split is exact to the cent.

What it does

Invoices

  • Scan to extract vendor, invoice number, dates, currency, printed total and line items
  • A totals check against the printed total
  • Editable fields, with rescans that never overwrite edits
  • Payment tracking with overdue detection

Dashboard and export

  • Outstanding, overdue and paid-this-month amounts, kept separate per currency
  • Search, and filters by status and category
  • CSV export of the filtered list, with cells escaped so text from a scanned document can’t run as a spreadsheet formula

Splitting a bill

  • Each item split equally, by percentage or by custom amount
  • Percentages must add up to 100% and custom amounts to the item’s total before they’re accepted
  • Tax shared evenly across everyone
  • A breakdown of which items make up each person’s share

Accounts

  • Passwords hashed with bcrypt
  • 15-minute access tokens and 7-day refresh tokens
  • Every query scoped to the signed-in user

Decisions and trade-offs

The LLM is a first pass, not the answer

Even at low temperature the model isn’t deterministic, so its output goes through a cleanup layer that can be tested on its own. Each fix there came from a real receipt that went wrong.

OCR plus a text model, not a vision model

Local OCR costs nothing per call, and its output is predictable, which makes failures easy to see and fix. The model then only does what text models are good at: structuring.

Totals that always reconcile

Splitting and rounding can leave a cent unaccounted for. A final pass compares the sum of everyone’s share with the bill’s grand total and gives any difference to the largest share, so the numbers always add up. Results are saved with insert-or-replace, so recalculating is safe.

What I’d change at scale

Flask handles OCR and model calls synchronously, which ties up a worker, and SQLite allows one writer at a time. For more users I’d move to an async job queue and PostgreSQL.
