What it’s for

The Income Tax Act is hundreds of pages of dense legal text. Most people only want to know what applies to them, and they need to see where the answer comes from before they can rely on it.

SA Tax Bot answers questions about the Act in plain language, and every point of the answer carries a citation that opens the exact passage it came from, with the section, its line numbers and its Government Gazette page. Points it can’t support aren’t shown.

It also ships the offline tooling behind it: turning the scanned Act into text, building the search index, fine-tuning a local model on the Act, and scanning a bank statement for transactions that look like deductions. It’s informational only, not tax advice, and the interface says so.

How an answer is made

  1. The Act becomes searchable text

    The scanned PDF of the Act is run through Tesseract OCR with PyMuPDF. Every line is tagged with its section, its line number and its Gazette page.

  2. It’s indexed

    The text is split into paragraphs, embedded with all-MiniLM-L6-v2 and stored in a ChromaDB vector index.

  3. A question finds the right provisions

    Retrieval combines vector search with sections whose titles closely match the question, and skips table-of-contents chunks that match well but say nothing.

  4. A local model answers

    A Hugging Face model, Qwen2.5-7B Instruct in my setup, answers from those excerpts only. On an NVIDIA GPU it loads in 4-bit to fit in about 6 GB of memory, and a Stop button cancels generation mid-answer.

  5. Every point is checked for a source

    The answer is split into points, and each one is matched by embedding similarity to the passages that were actually retrieved. A point that names a section can only be cited to that section’s text. A point with no close match is hidden rather than given a made-up citation.

What it does

Chat

  • Plain-language answers about the Act
  • A citation on every point, opening the source lines
  • Saved conversations with search, per user
  • A Stop button that cancels generation on the server

Two ways to answer

  • Retrieval mode, the default, answers from the indexed Act
  • Fine-tuned mode uses a LoRA adapter trained on the Act instead of retrieval
  • One setting switches between them

Training pipeline

  • A raw text corpus of the Act for causal language-model pretraining
  • An instruction-tuning dataset built from the Act
  • LoRA fine-tuning of a local model with PEFT
  • A terminal chat for testing without the web app

Deduction scanner

  • Generates a realistic South African bank statement for testing
  • Sends only money going out to the model, with relevant passages of the Act
  • Flags transactions that look like deductions
  • Marks a batch for manual review if the model’s output isn’t valid JSON

Decisions and trade-offs

Citations are checked, not generated

Asking a model to cite its sources invites invented section numbers. Instead, citations are attached after the answer by matching each point against the retrieved text. If nothing is close enough, the point is dropped rather than shown with a citation that looks trustworthy and isn’t.

A local model

Answers come from a model running on your own machine, so questions about personal finances never leave it. The trade-off is speed: without a GPU, a 7B model is slow and needs about 16 GB of memory.

Retrieval and fine-tuning, side by side

Both approaches run in the same app, so they can be compared on the same questions. Retrieval stays the default because it can point to its source; a fine-tuned model can’t.

Known limit

The text is the Act as published in 1962, so later amendments aren’t included. The interface states this under every chat.
